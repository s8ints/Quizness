Barringer & Ireland · Chapter 4

Business model

We propose a primarily standard business model, led by recurring institutional licences. Optional student subscriptions and paid-tutoring commissions would be later experiments. [9]

Why standard, rather than disruptive?

Chapter 4 lists freemium and subscriptions among standard models. Quizzness would use these established methods to create, deliver and capture value. [1, pp. 150–153, Table 4.1]

Our proposed difference connects reviewed course practice to screened peer support and approved service-hour records. We have no evidence that this changes an industry’s competitive logic or creates a difficult-to-copy business model, so a disruptive classification is not justified.

How value would flow

01

Create value

Provide course challenges, explanations and retries, then connect persistent difficulties to focused peer help.

02

Deliver value

Deliver browser activities through institution partnerships. Partners would review content, screen tutors and oversee eligible GiveBack sessions.

03

Capture value

Test semester licences for institutions providing learner access. Later, test optional premium practice and a commission on paid tutoring. Paid sessions would be separate from approved volunteer service.

Course comparison: CoachUp

Chapter 4 uses CoachUp to illustrate creating, delivering and capturing value by connecting athletes with private coaches. [1, p. 150]

Quizzness proposes a related support-matching mechanism for courses, but institutions would fund the initial offer and oversee volunteer service sessions. A commission would apply only to optional paid tutoring. This comparison explains value delivery and capture; it does not predict our success.

What the survey says about access

18 of 28 respondents (64.3%) preferred free access with optional premium features, while 4 (14.3%) selected a low-cost monthly subscription. This informs a possible later student tier; it does not validate prices or institutional demand. [8, Q20]

What must be true for this to work?

Income would need to cover development, hosting, content review, tutor screening, coordination and support. Reusable templates could spread activity-development effort, but human support still depends on capacity.

Before setting a price, we would estimate cost per course and per active student, test paid interest and check whether recurring income exceeds recurring delivery costs. Until then, profitability is an assumption.
