Barringer & Ireland · Chapter 3

Feasibility analysis

Our judgement is a conditional go for a small pilot. Demand, learning outcomes and commercial viability remain unverified.

Concept statement

For tertiary learners in Barbados who need course practice outside class, Quizzness would combine reviewed minigames, explanatory feedback and topic-focused peer support. Institutions would provide access and oversee tutors and eligible GiveBack service hours. Two reviewed courses would be the initial pilot scope. [9]

Product/service feasibility

Chapter 3 separates desirability from demand: an appealing idea still needs evidence that people will use or buy it. [1, pp. 110–118]

Desirability: plausible

Retrieval-practice research found better comprehension and inference performance than concept mapping in the studied science-learning tasks. This supports practice questions, but does not establish that our minigames improve university results. [2]

Demand: early interest, not adoption

21 of 28 respondents (75.0%) said they would definitely or probably practise more with a game-like experience. 24 (85.7%) rated their likelihood of trying the described platform 4 or 5. This is stated interest after a favourable concept description, not observed adoption. [8, Q11] [8, Q18]

What our survey supports

26 respondents (92.9%) rated identifying weak topics 4 or 5; 27 (96.4%) gave those ratings to targeted practice questions and immediate explanations. 19 (67.9%) wanted tutor or peer help, with another 8 unsure. This points toward testing feedback and focused support, rather than concentrating only on game rewards. [8, Q14] [8, Q15] [8, Q16] [8, Q17]

Gamification research found positive learning-related effects with variation across studies. EEF identifies structured tasks and tutor training as important in school peer tutoring. Neither source establishes the effectiveness of this tertiary platform. [10] [11]

Delivery risk: converting notes into accurate application questions may require substantial lecturer review. If AI is used later, UNESCO’s guidance supports pedagogical validation and attention to privacy. Our proposed response is human review before publishing an activity. [5]

Industry/target-market feasibility

Chapter 3 asks whether both the industry and the specific target market are attractive. A narrow entry market should be assessed separately from the broader industry. [1, pp. 118–122]

Industry: Quizlet and Kahoot! already provide independent practice. Our proposed position connects reviewed course practice to coordinated peer support; we must test whether that adds value. [3] [4]

Target market: 17 of the 28 survey respondents were bachelor's students, but recruitment and location are not documented. We cannot use this sample to estimate the Barbados market. Access to a partner cohort, course-material permissions, buyer budgets and tutor capacity remain assumptions. [8, Q1]

Assumptions, evidence gaps and proposed decision rules

All participant counts and thresholds are proposed project targets, not industry benchmarks or results.

Decision

Proceed with a limited prototype and course pilot. Revise or stop if repeat use is low, content-review effort is excessive or buyers cannot fund delivery. Do not scale solely because students enjoy the games.
