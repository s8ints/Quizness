01 / THE OPPORTUNITY

The notes are everywhere.
The understanding shouldn’t be.

Our sample starting point: students need a way to turn scattered course material into focused practice they can trust.

01

The problem

Imagine a study group switching between chat threads, shared notes, and generic quizzes. Finding relevant practice becomes another task on the to-do list.

02

The people

Our proposed first audience is university students and peer tutors studying the same introductory courses. A small course community is the starting point.

03

The possibility

A shared question bank connects each question to a course topic and a peer explanation. More useful contributions could improve practice for everyone.

Problem and demand are hypotheses. Interviews and observation are needed before treating them as evidence.

WHY NOW

More content. A greater need for context.

Study platforms already offer AI-generated practice materials, as illustrated by Quizlet and Kahoot!. Our inference: course communities may benefit from visible question provenance and accountable review. This is a timing hypothesis, not evidence of demand for Quizness.

Initial use case: a first-year student preparing for an introductory-course exam, with a peer tutor helping review practice questions.
