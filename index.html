01 / THE OPPORTUNITY

The notes are everywhere.
The understanding shouldn’t be.

Our sample starting point: students need a way to turn scattered course material into focused practice they can trust.

01

The problem

Imagine a study group switching between chat threads, shared notes, and generic quizzes. Finding relevant practice becomes another task on the to-do list.

02

The people

Our proposed first audience is university students and peer tutors studying the same introductory courses. A small course community is the starting point.

03

The possibility

A shared question bank connects each question to a course topic and a peer explanation. More useful contributions could improve practice for everyone.

Problem and demand are hypotheses. Interviews and observation are needed before treating them as evidence.

03 / FEASIBILITY & EVIDENCE

A promising hypothesis.
A plan to put it to the test.

Concept statement: a course-based network for student-created quizzes, with explanations and a proposed peer review process.

FICTIONAL RESEARCH EXAMPLE
18/24

Invented example only. No interviews were conducted and no demand has been validated.

Sample pilot target: 2 course circles, 40 learners, and 60 reviewed questions. These are planning examples, not actual traction.

↗ Product / service feasibility

The essential flow can be demonstrated with a short quiz and feedback. Test whether students can find relevant questions and understand the explanations.

↗ Industry / target-market feasibility

Start with one university course community. Investigate willingness to contribute, tutor availability, existing study habits, and willingness to pay.

↗ Our provisional judgement

Suitable for a focused prototype; commercial feasibility is unproven. Content accuracy, participation, and moderation effort are the biggest assumptions.

Explore the industry assumptions & Five Forces +

Rivalry & substitutes

Free study tools and familiar workflows could be hard to displace. Investigate whether course relevance provides enough reason to switch.

New entrants

A simple quiz interface is easy to imitate. Test whether useful reviewed content and active course circles create a stronger advantage.

Buyer power

Students may prefer free alternatives. Validate the fictional price and premium value before building paid features.

Supplier power

Contributors and peer tutors supply the content and review effort. Investigate participation incentives, ownership, and moderation costs.

Industry trend hypotheses

Explore demand for mobile practice, course communities, and content transparency. These are research topics, not verified trends.
