THE QUIZNESS PROPOSAL / 03

Test the idea.

Quizness concept, feasibility, evidence, assumptions, and a proposed pilot.

03 / FEASIBILITY & EVIDENCE

A promising hypothesis.
A plan to put it to the test.

Concept statement: a course-based network for student-created quizzes, with explanations and a proposed peer review process.

EVIDENCE STATUS

Learning rationale supported.
Venture demand untested.

Retrieval Practice’s research guidance explains how recalling information can support learning. This supports the practice mechanism; it does not establish that students want Quizness or will pay for it.

Current product evidence: the static quiz preview demonstrates information layout, not a working service or learning outcome.

Proposed pilot: 2 course circles, 40 learners, and 60 reviewed questions. No pilot or interviews have been conducted.

↗ Product / service feasibility

The essential flow can be demonstrated with a short quiz and feedback. Test whether students can find relevant questions and understand the explanations.

↗ Industry / target-market feasibility

Start with one university course community. Investigate willingness to contribute, tutor availability, existing study habits, and willingness to pay.

↗ Our provisional judgement

Suitable for a focused prototype; commercial feasibility is unproven. Content accuracy, participation, and moderation effort are the biggest assumptions.

ASSUMPTIONS & VALIDATION

Decide what would change our minds.

Illustrative decision criteria for a four-week pilot; these are targets, not results.

PROPOSED TESTS · NO COLLECTED RESULTS
Assumption Test Decision criterion
Course practice solves a recurring pain Interview 12 students and 3 peer tutors about recent study sessions. At least 8 students describe a recent difficulty finding relevant practice.
Learners can use the workflow Observe 5 students finding a topic and interpreting feedback. At least 4 complete both tasks without assistance.
Reviewed content is sustainable Log tutor time and independently check a sample of reviewed questions. Average review time under 10 minutes; no unresolved correctness errors in the checked sample.
Practice becomes a repeat habit Track consented, minimal pilot usage. At least half the learners return in week two.
Premium value can cover costs Test the proposed price in interviews and estimate hosting, support, and review costs. Advance only if a realistic paid-conversion scenario covers recurring costs.

Implementation needs course membership, a question database, answer feedback, a review queue, and reporting tools. Limit the first build to one course; obtain permission for source material and define reviewer responsibilities before inviting learners.
