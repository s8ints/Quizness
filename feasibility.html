Barringer & Ireland · Chapter 3

Promising enough to test.
Too early to scale.

Our judgement is a conditional go for a small pilot. Demand, learning outcomes and commercial viability remain unverified.

Concept statement

For students who follow lectures but struggle with independent application, Quizzness would convert selected course material into short quiz minigames with explanations and topic-based practice. Our proposed first market is first-year programming and mathematics students at one university. Lecturer-reviewed activities would be delivered through a browser, with paid features considered only after a pilot.

Product/service feasibility

Chapter 3 separates desirability from demand: an appealing idea still needs evidence that people will use or buy it. [1, pp. 110–118]

Desirability: plausible

Retrieval-practice research found better comprehension and inference performance than concept mapping in the studied science-learning tasks. This supports practice questions, but does not establish that our minigames improve university results. [2]

Demand: not established

Quizlet already offers study activities and material-to-question tools. Existing alternatives show an established product category, not demand for Quizzness. We need concept interviews and observed repeat use. [3]

Delivery risk: converting notes into accurate application questions may require substantial lecturer review. If AI is used later, UNESCO’s guidance supports pedagogical validation and attention to privacy. Our proposed response is human review before publishing an activity. [5]

Industry/target-market feasibility

Chapter 3 asks whether both the industry and the specific target market are attractive. A narrow entry market should be assessed separately from the broader industry. [1, pp. 118–122]

Industry: digital study tools offer a feasible route to a prototype, but Quizlet and Kahoot! already provide independent practice. Our inference is that rivalry and substitutes limit the appeal of a generic quiz product. [3] [4]

Target market: one introductory course gives us a proposed route to students and reviewers. Access to that cohort, permission to use material, affordability and willingness to pay are assumptions, not secured commitments. No market-size estimate or student survey is claimed.

Proposed validation · not completed research

Assumptions, evidence gaps and proposed decision rules
AssumptionTestPlanning threshold
The problem mattersInterview 12 students and 2 lecturers about recent independent-practice difficulties.Identify repeated problems and test whether the concept addresses them.
The journey is clearAsk 5 students to choose, answer and review a sample topic.At least 4 complete the journey without assistance.
Students returnInvite 30 consenting students to a four-week pilot.At least half return during week two.
Practice helps applicationCompare baseline and follow-up application tasks with a notes-only comparison group.Look for improvement beyond the comparison; a small pilot cannot establish a definitive effect.

All participant counts and thresholds are proposed project targets, not industry benchmarks or results.

Decision

Proceed with a limited prototype and course pilot. Revise or stop if repeat use is low, content-review effort is excessive or buyers cannot fund delivery. Do not scale solely because students enjoy the games.
