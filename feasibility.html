THE PROPOSAL / FEASIBILITY

Start small.
Test the complete loop.

One university, two courses, approved game content and a manageable peer-support programme.

CONCEPT & EVIDENCE

A course world connected to real support.

Quizzness is a proposed gamified university learning platform that links course adventures and topic-level practice to screened and verified peer tutors and institution-reviewed GiveBack records.

EVIDENCE STATUS

The journey is demonstrated.
The venture is unvalidated.

The tree puzzle demonstrates interaction and feedback; the tutor view demonstrates requests and verification states. Neither proves learning outcomes, demand or service-hour recognition.

Retrieval Practice research guidance supports recalling information as a learning mechanism. It does not validate these adventure mechanics or the proposed business.

Team evidence rationale: the trend and popularity of gamification-based learning and demand for affordable tutoring. Duolingo’s teaching-method overview provides an example of gamified learning and repeated practice. Evidence of local tutoring demand and institutional interest still needs cited research or interviews; no demand measurements have been supplied.

Proposed pilot: 50–100 students, 10 peer tutors and 20–30 games per course across Programming and Mathematics. These are scope targets; no pilot has occurred.

Product / service feasibility

Interactive games and a beneficial tutoring system provide additional out-of-class practice of course material. Games remain work in progress; progress saving, tracking and engaging repetition are planned parts of the service. Reusable templates and lecturer review are a proposed implementation approach.

Industry / target-market feasibility

The team’s target-market rationale is that universities seek student friend/peer-support systems and platforms. Universities and colleges are potential institutional buyers. Validate interest, available budgets, course needs, tutor screening and GiveBack recognition with prospective partners.

Provisional judgement

A scoped prototype is feasible; delivery and commercial success remain unproven. Content accuracy, tutor quality, formal GiveBack recognition and repeat use are the main risks.

ASSUMPTIONS & VALIDATION

Test learning, support and recognition together.
