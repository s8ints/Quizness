Industry and competitors

Industry trends

Gamification, digital study tools and peer learning are relevant to our idea. Duolingo, Quizlet and Kahoot! show different approaches to practice and engagement.

These examples do not tell us how much local demand there is. We still need research with students and university staff.

Competitor comparison

We compared four study products and one everyday alternative. Duolingo is a comparison for game-based practice, even though its main audience differs from ours.

Porter's Five Forces

Using the industry-analysis framework in Barringer and Ireland, Chapter 5, these are the questions we need to consider:

1. Substitutes

Could students get enough help from study groups or existing tools? They may not switch unless our feedback and tutor access are useful.

2. Rivalry

How would we compete with other study tools? We would focus on course relevance and the connection to peer support.

3. Supplier power

How dependent would we be on lecturers, tutors and universities? Their time, course materials and approval would affect what we can offer.

4. Buyer power

What would universities and students pay for? Buyers could compare us with free or established alternatives and negotiate prices.

5. New entrants

Could another provider copy the idea? Practice interfaces may be easy to copy. Reliable university partnerships, reviewed content and recognised GiveBack processes could take longer to build.

These are initial judgements. Interviews and the pilot should help us check them. Exact textbook edition and page references are still needed.
