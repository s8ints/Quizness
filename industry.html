THE QUIZZNESS PROPOSAL / 05

Understand the alternatives.

Study-tool trends, a competitor grid, and all five competitive forces.

INDUSTRY & COMPETITION

Course relevance must earn its place.

The initial market is university course communities using digital study tools. The comparison below covers four named learning products and one everyday substitute. Duolingo is a gamification comparison; product audiences and institutional fit differ.

INDUSTRY TRENDS

Gamification. Edtech. Peer learning.

The team identifies these three directions as relevant to the venture. Product examples illustrate the approaches; they do not establish local demand or market growth.

Gamification

Duolingo’s teaching method uses interactive activities and game elements to encourage repeated practice. Quizzness is developing games to apply course knowledge and retain information.

Educational technology

Quizlet and Kahoot! illustrate digital practice and study tools. Quizzness proposes combining activities, feedback and progress tracking in a course-focused platform.

Peer-based learning

The team’s opportunity is to organise tutors and learners who may currently connect through informal groups. Screened tutoring and GiveBack records provide the proposed structure. Evidence of institutional adoption and demand still needs to be collected.

FIVE FORCES

Five questions before we scale.

Provisional assessments for the proposed university-student market. Interviews and pilot evidence should update each judgement.

1. Substitutes

Question: Can learners meet their needs through informal WhatsApp groups, in-person study groups or Quizlet?

These alternatives may be familiar and affordable. Test whether structured feedback, engaging repetition and screened peer tutoring justify switching.

2. Rivalry

Question: How does Quizzness compete with other educational technology?

Practice and gamification are already offered by other products. Evaluate the value of combining course activities, feedback, tutor access and GiveBack tracking.

3. Supplier power

Question: How dependent is the platform on universities, colleges and lecturers?

These partners can supply or approve course materials, academic expertise and programme access. Their participation and terms affect content availability, tutor screening and GiveBack recognition.

4. Buyer power

Question: What would students, universities and colleges be willing to pay for?

Institutions are the primary B2B2C buyers; students are potential buyers of premiums and paid tutoring. Each can compare alternatives and influence price, access and feature expectations.

5. Threat of new entrants

Question: How easily could another provider reproduce the idea?

Game and quiz interfaces can be copied. Partner relationships, approved content, reliable screening and recognised GiveBack processes may be harder to establish. This fifth force is a provisional assessment to complete the framework.
