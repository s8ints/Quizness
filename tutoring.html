Peer tutoring

If a student is still stuck after practising, they could find a peer tutor for that course. Tutors would need to meet the university's eligibility rules and complete training.

Sample tutor

Marcus Clarke

Course: Programming (sample COMP 101 course)
Topics: binary tree insertion, traversal and deletion

Marcus is a fictional tutor used for this example. In the proposed service, a coordinator would check course results, training and the tutor's agreement to the code of conduct.

Example session request

The student needs help with binary search tree deletion. They could request a short explanation or a longer worked example.

15-minute explanation

Example request: 15 minutes with Marcus on a weekday afternoon, covering tree deletion.

The tutor would accept the request or suggest another time.

30-minute worked example

Example request: 30 minutes with Marcus on a weekday evening, working through tree deletion step by step.

The tutor would accept the request or suggest another time.

These are previews only. Opening a request does not send a booking. Peer GiveBack sessions would be free to students in the proposed university programme.

After a session

For a separate example, imagine that the student and Marcus have finished a 30-minute session on deletion. They discussed how to replace a node that has two children.

Both people would confirm the session time. The student could give feedback, and the tutor could send the time for coordinator review.

The wallet would keep a record of tutoring time. Pending time would be separate from approved time.

How time would be approved

  1. The student and tutor confirm the session.
  2. A coordinator checks the time and programme rules.
  3. Approved time is added to the tutor's verified total.
  4. The university issues a report or certificate under its rules.

We would need a formal university agreement before claiming that tutoring counts toward GiveBack requirements. The totals above are sample data.
