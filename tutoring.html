Peer tutoring

If a student is still stuck after practising, they could find a peer tutor for that course. Tutors would need to meet the university's eligibility rules and complete training. Before launching this service, we would need to test whether it can work.

“Feasibility analysis is the process of determining if a business idea is viable.”

(Barringer & Ireland, 2019, Ch. 3, p. 109)

All tutors, requests, sessions, and wallet records on this page are fictional prototype examples. No personal information is collected and no booking is made.

Want to help other students? Apply to become a peer tutor and work toward school GiveBack hours.

After a session

For a separate fictional completed-session example, imagine that the student and Marcus have finished a 30-minute session on equations. They practised doing the same operation to both sides of an equation.

Both people would confirm the session time. The student could give feedback, and the tutor could send the time for coordinator review.

Research supports this kind of structured help. A meta-analysis of 37 studies found a moderately strong positive relationship between formal peer learning approaches and course performance in higher education (Bengesai et al., 2023).

The pending 0.5 hours are not part of the 24 approved hours. These fixed sample records do not change when you fill in the sample forms or refresh the page.

How time would be approved

  1. The student and tutor confirm the session.
  2. A coordinator checks the time and programme rules.
  3. Approved time is added to the tutor's verified total.
  4. The university issues a report or certificate under its rules.

We would need a formal university agreement before claiming that tutoring counts toward GiveBack requirements. The totals above are sample data.

No pilot results are claimed; GiveBack recognition requires formal approval by a university partner.
