Venture status: Quizzness is a university startup proposal and front-end prototype. No launched service, university partnership, or GiveBack recognition is claimed.

A proposed learning business

Quizzness would turn university coursework into short game-based learning activities, connect students to useful explanations, and help them find screened peer tutors. Universities and colleges would be potential institutional customers, while students and peer tutors would use the platform.

Our proposed mission

Help university students practise difficult concepts, understand their mistakes, and find an appropriate next step when they need support.

The problem we aim to solve

Following a classroom explanation does not always translate into independent practice. Students may struggle to identify a mistake or arrange timely help through informal study groups and chats.

Our proposed connection is coursework → practice → feedback → peer support → retry. Tutor GiveBack time would require session confirmation, coordinator review, and a formal university agreement.

Our next step

Complete and improve the prototype using team labour and agreed personal contributions. Seek a suitable business or educational-institution partner, then invite students and tutors to test the experience and provide feedback.

Use that feedback to guide more detailed development. Agree a budget, safety arrangements, course permissions and institutional responsibilities before offering a live service.

Agreements, safety and ownership

The team is considering a limited-liability company or corporation in the future; Quizzness is not yet formally established. The report proposes a founders' agreement and code of conduct so responsibilities and future equity can be discussed and recorded fairly.

The team intends to clarify ownership of its work, investigate protection for the Quizzness name, and use suitable confidentiality agreements when outsourcing. Course permissions, asset rights, data-protection requirements and tutor/volunteer policies would need agreement before launch. These are plans, rather than completed registrations or permissions.
