Puzzle Temple: maths practice

This is a sample of how a course activity could work. Learn the idea, try a quest and use the feedback to decide what to practise next.

1. Learn the idea

A percentage means “per hundred.” To find 20% of an amount, write 20/100 = 0.20 and multiply the amount by 0.20. (OpenStax, 2020, 6.1)

Example: 20% of BBD 40 = 0.20 × 40 = BBD 8.

B. BBD 10: Correct

  1. Convert 20% to a decimal: 20 ÷ 100 = 0.20.
  2. Multiply: 0.20 × 50 = 10.
  3. The answer is BBD 10.

C. BBD 20: Try again

20% is a proportion, not a fixed currency amount. Multiply 0.20 by the amount given. BBD 20 would be 40% of BBD 50.

3. Practise the same idea

What is 25% of BBD 80?

  • A. BBD 10
  • B. BBD 20
  • C. BBD 25

A. BBD 10: Try again

BBD 10 is one eighth of 80, or 12.5%. For 25%, find one quarter of 80.

Retry | See the worked answer

B. BBD 20: Correct

25 ÷ 100 = 0.25. Then 0.25 × 80 = 20. You can also divide 80 by 4 because 25% is one quarter. (OpenStax, 2020, §6.1)

C. BBD 25: Try again

The percentage is 25%, not BBD 25. Apply it to the amount: 0.25 × 80 = 20.

Course topics

We would organise each course into small activities. These are our planned areas for Puzzle Temple:

  1. Percent Path: percentages (sample above)
  2. Fraction Falls: fractions
  3. Ratio Bridge: ratios and proportion
  4. Equation Tower: solving equations
  5. Final challenge: questions from different topics

Adding more activities

We plan to use question and game templates. A lecturer or approved creator would add course material, and a coordinator would check the questions and explanations before students use them.

Like Duolingo’s repeated practice model, Quizzness uses short interactive challenges to reinforce learning. Unlike Quizlet, Quizzness connects weak topics directly to peer tutoring.

How this prototype is built

This page uses plain HTML for its structure and one external CSS file for its appearance.

“The purpose of HTML is to add meaning and structure to the content.”

(Robbins, 2018, Ch. 4, p. 59)

Style sheets show “the power in keeping CSS separate from HTML, and presentation separate from structure.”

(Robbins, 2018, Ch. 11, p. 240)

The pages also adjust to narrow screens using a flexible width, scalable images and a media query.

“Media queries give us a way to deliver sets of rules only to devices that meet certain criteria, such as width and orientation.”

(Robbins, 2018, Ch. 17, p. 487)

The full service would need accounts and progress tracking. Those features are not part of this website yet.
