Barringer & Ireland · Chapter 4

A familiar model.
A focused learning service.

We propose a primarily standard business model: free basic practice, optional premium subscriptions and institutional course licences.

Why standard, rather than disruptive?

Chapter 4 lists freemium and subscriptions among standard models. Quizzness would use these established methods to create, deliver and capture value. [1, pp. 150–153, Table 4.1]

Our proposed difference is reviewed practice aligned to a particular course, with minigame templates and explanations. We have no evidence that this changes an industry’s competitive logic or creates a difficult-to-copy business model, so a disruptive classification is not justified.

How value would flow

01

Create value

Convert selected course material into short application questions and explanatory feedback. Reuse game templates while adapting the content to each topic.

02

Deliver value

Offer browser-based practice on phones and computers. A course partner would provide approved material and review questions; students would choose a topic and practise independently.

03

Capture value

Keep basic activities free. Test subscriptions for extra course packs and progress tools, plus semester licences for institutions. Pricing and buyer demand remain untested.

Course comparison: Dropbox

Barringer and Ireland use Dropbox as a freemium example: basic access is free and a premium version earns revenue. [1, p. 152, Table 4.1]

Quizzness would similarly lower the barrier to trying the service with free basic practice. Its proposed paid value is additional reviewed course content and study tools, rather than file storage. Institutional licences would add a second buyer route. The comparison explains our revenue structure; it does not establish that Dropbox’s success would transfer to Quizzness.

What must be true for this to work?

Income would need to cover development, hosting, content review and support. Reusable templates could spread development effort across courses, but reviewing new course content remains a recurring task.

Before setting a price, we would estimate cost per course and per active student, test paid interest and check whether recurring income exceeds recurring delivery costs. Until then, profitability is an assumption.
