Business model

How we would earn money

Our main model is B2B2C: a university pays for platform access, and its students use the service. We would discuss a licence based on participating students per semester.

Proposed primary revenue

Institutional licences

Universities and colleges would pay for access to reviewed course activities and a coordinated peer-support service. Pricing, scope, and responsibilities would need agreement with a prospective customer.

Optional secondary revenue

Student subscriptions

Optional extra activities or features could be considered later. Premium features, student willingness to pay, and the boundary with institution-funded access have not been decided.

Possible later option

Paid tutoring commissions

A commission could be considered for separate paid tutoring sessions. Rates and operating rules are undecided. Paid tutoring would remain separate from free peer GiveBack sessions.

Optional subscriptions and commissions should not be counted as assured income when assessing whether an institutional licence could sustain the core service.

Creating, delivering and capturing value

“A firm’s business model is its plan or recipe for how it creates, delivers, and captures value for its stakeholders.”

(Barringer & Ireland, 2019, Ch. 4, p. 149)

Create

Help students practise coursework, understand feedback, and find suitable peer help when a topic remains difficult.

Deliver

Provide course activities and tutoring access through a proposed university partnership. Content approval and tutor oversight would need agreed responsibilities.

Capture

Use proposed licence income to support development, hosting, content review, and support, with optional secondary revenue only if validated.

Standard or disruptive?

We classify Quizzness as a standard licensing and subscription model. The combination of course games and peer tutoring is our proposed difference, but we do not have evidence that it would disrupt the market.

This applies the standard and disruptive business-model concepts in Barringer and Ireland (2019, Ch. 4, pp. 151–154).

Comparison with Duolingo

Duolingo uses interactive lessons, repeated practice and game elements. We want to use practice activities for university coursework.

Duolingo offers free learning with optional premium subscriptions. This is similar to our secondary subscription idea. Our main proposed income would come from institutions rather than individual subscriptions.

Optional student subscriptions are a secondary possibility, not the main funding assumption. Our proposed primary revenue is institutional licensing; a sustainable service cannot be assumed from a freemium comparison alone.

Quizzness would also connect course feedback to peer tutoring and GiveBack records. We would need to test whether students and universities value that connection. Recognised GiveBack time would require a formal university agreement.

What would make the business sustainable?

  1. Estimate the real workload and costs for a limited course offering before agreeing a licence price.
  2. Test whether a potential institution values the service enough to fund those costs.
  3. Review repeat participation, tutor capacity, content quality, and support workload before renewal or expansion.

Before building the full service, we would estimate these costs with a prospective partner and check whether licence income could cover them. If the model cannot support reliable delivery, the scope or pricing would need revision.

As Barringer and Ireland (2019, Ch. 3) note, feasibility analysis tests whether a business idea is viable. For Quizzness, that depends on content accuracy, tutor quality, institutional support and student retention. These are our main risks.
