THE PROPOSAL / BUSINESS MODEL

A university partner.
A supported student.

A proposed B2B2C platform: universities license the service, students use it, and eligible peer tutors provide support.

VALUE CREATION / DELIVERY / CAPTURE

Learn together. Give back.

Primary: B2B2C access through universities and colleges. Secondary: student subscriptions for premium features. Tertiary: tutoring payments and commissions. The connected learning, feedback and peer-support workflow creates the proposed value.

CREATE VALUE

Turn difficulty into a next step

Course-approved games support application, topic feedback suggests where to practise, and eligible tutors provide focused help. Service records give tutors a reviewable account of their contribution.

DELIVER VALUE

Through university partnerships

Partner accounts connect course libraries, student dashboards, tutor eligibility, session management and GiveBack review. A lecturer-led template builder supports content creation without automatic AI generation.

CAPTURE VALUE

License the support system

The proposed primary revenue is a university licence per participating student per semester. Peer GiveBack sessions remain free to students in the proposed partner programme.

Institutional access + optional premiumsPricing and commission rates to be confirmed

Classification: a standard licensing/subscription model. Combining games, peer tutoring and service-hour tracking is a proposed innovation; disruptive-market impact has not been established.

COURSE CONCEPT COMPARISON

Who pays changes how the network grows.

Compare standard revenue models using value creation, delivery and capture. Verify this concept-level analysis against the assigned Barringer & Ireland Chapter 4 case.

B2B2C · primary

Universities or colleges purchase platform access for their students. The institution is the business customer and students are the end users. Institutional agreements define access, peer-support arrangements and programme responsibilities.

Student premiums · secondary

Students can subscribe for premium benefits such as additional activities or practice options. The feature split and price remain to be confirmed, including which benefits are already covered by institutional access.

Tutoring pay & commissions · tertiary

Paid tutoring provides tutor income and a potential platform commission. Payment rules, rates and commission percentages remain to be confirmed. Keep paid sessions distinct from institution-recognised GiveBack sessions.

MARKET COMPARISON / DUOLINGO

Engaging practice, with a peer-support connection.

The team identifies Duolingo as a useful comparison for gamification and repeated learning activities.

Similar learning approach

Duolingo describes interactive lessons, personalised practice, points and streaks. Quizzness proposes engaging repetition, feedback and progress tracking for course material; its game formats are still being developed.

Premium comparison

Duolingo offers free learning and optional premium subscriptions. This is comparable to Quizzness’s secondary student-premium idea. Quizzness’s primary model remains B2B2C institutional access.

Proposed Quizzness focus

Quizzness centres on courses and subjects, screened peer tutors and institution-recognised GiveBack hours. Similar mechanics do not establish equivalent learning outcomes or business success.

ECONOMIC ASSUMPTIONS

A small licence needs a real cost model.

Revenue priorities are confirmed; prices, premium benefits and commission rates remain to be decided.

Revenue scenario

Institutional revenue depends on agreed access fees and participating students; premium revenue depends on paying subscribers; tutoring commission depends on paid session value and the agreed platform percentage. No confirmed price or forecast is presented.

Cost drivers

Game-template development, approved content production, tutor training, verification, hosting, support and institutional onboarding. Volunteer tutor time still needs coordination.

Decision gate

Build a priced pilot scope with the university. Proceed only when licence income can reasonably cover ongoing delivery and peer-support administration.
