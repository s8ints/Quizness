How the proposed venture would sustain its service

Business model

Institutional licensing would fund the core service; optional revenue streams would need separate validation.

Proposed model: prices and sales are not confirmed. The final report includes preliminary costs and revenue scenarios, not achieved results. This prototype does not take payments or provide paid services.

How the venture would earn money

The proposed model is B2B2C: institutions pay for annual licences and students use the platform. Institutions would agree content review, tutor screening and support. Small advertisements may provide extra income if institutions agree and they do not interrupt learning.

Pricing and financial assumptions

The report assumes BBD $50 per student annually for an institutional licence. It also models 12 advertisement-eligible page views per student each month, at an assumed BBD $4 per 1,000 views. These are planning assumptions, not agreed prices or guaranteed advertising rates.

Premium features and tutoring commissions are excluded from the projections because their prices and demand are untested. These options would need cost review and agreement with possible partners.

A standard venture with a focused difference

Quizzness is proposed as a standard venture. It combines familiar methods such as gamification, online practice and peer support around course material supplied or approved by educators. The report does not claim that this would fundamentally disrupt the market.

The core strategy is course-specific practice, immediate explanations and a direct path to screened peer support. Institutional partnerships and prototype feedback would guide expansion (Barringer & Ireland, 2019, Chapter 4).

Comparison with Duolingo

Duolingo uses interactive lessons, repeated practice and game elements. We want to use practice activities for university coursework.

The final report cites about US$1.04 billion in Duolingo's 2025 revenue, including about US$873.4 million from subscriptions and US$79.7 million from advertising. This shows possible income routes for an established platform, but Duolingo is much larger and mainly sells individual subscriptions. Quizzness cannot assume similar results. Resources, costs and initial funding

Isaiah proposed bootstrapping and sweat equity, and the team agreed. The report values about 122 hours of overall project work at BBD $20 per hour, or BBD $2,440. This includes research, writing, development and testing. It is unpaid work, not cash spent.

No actual money has been invested or spent. Any later contributions would be voluntary, limited, agreed and recorded. The prototype resource estimate is BBD $2,462.34, including unpaid labour and an assumed BBD $22.34 annual domain cost; a purchase is not claimed.

Building a sustainable service

Start within the team's available skills and resources. Use prototype feedback to improve the learning and tutoring journey, and estimate the real workload of reviewed content, software maintenance and support.

Institutional partnerships, innovation initiatives and grant opportunities are the first proposed routes to additional resources. Larger external financing would be considered only after the direction, costs and responsibilities are clearer.
