Business model

How we would earn money

Our main model is B2B2C: a university pays for platform access, and its students use the service. We would discuss a licence based on participating students per semester.

  • Main income: licences for universities and colleges.
  • Secondary income: optional student subscriptions for extra activities or features.
  • Later option: a commission on paid tutoring sessions.

Prices, premium features and commission rates have not been decided. Paid tutoring would be separate from free peer GiveBack sessions.

Creating, delivering and capturing value

Create: help students practise coursework, understand feedback and find suitable peer help.

Deliver: provide course activities and tutoring access through a university partnership. The university would approve content and oversee tutors.

Capture: earn licence and subscription income to pay for development, hosting, content review and support.

Standard or disruptive?

We classify Quizzness as a standard licensing and subscription model. The combination of course games and peer tutoring is our proposed difference, but we do not have evidence that it would disrupt the market.

This applies the business-model concepts in Barringer and Ireland, Chapter 4. We still need to add a comparison with the exact example assigned in class and its page reference.

Comparison with Duolingo

Duolingo uses interactive lessons, repeated practice and game elements. We want to use practice activities for university coursework.

Duolingo offers free learning with optional premium subscriptions. This is similar to our secondary subscription idea. Our main proposed income would come from institutions rather than individual subscriptions.

Quizzness would also connect course feedback to peer tutoring and GiveBack records. We would need to test whether students and universities value that connection.

Costs and risks

Our costs would include building activities, reviewing course content, training tutors, hosting the website and managing sessions. Volunteer tutoring would still need supervision.

Before building the full service, we would estimate these costs with a partner and check whether licence income could cover them.
