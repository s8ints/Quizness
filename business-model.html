Proposed model: there are no confirmed prices, sales, revenues, or financial projections. This prototype does not take payments or provide paid services.

How the venture would earn money

The primary proposed model is institutional licensing and partnerships. Educational institutions would provide access to students, with agreed arrangements for reviewed course content, tutor screening and support.

Institutional licensing

The main intended route to revenue is licences and partnerships with universities, colleges and other educational institutions. Prices, included support and responsibilities are not yet agreed.

Optional premium features

Additional premium activities or features are a future consideration. The team has not selected features or prices.

Tutor-related revenue

Tutor-related income is another future consideration. No payment structure, commission or rate has been agreed. Proposed GiveBack participation would depend on institutional approval.

Optional revenue streams are not confirmed income and should not be included as assured funding.

Creating, delivering and capturing value

“A firm’s business model is its plan or recipe for how it creates, delivers,and captures value for its stakeholders.”

(Barringer & Ireland, 2019, Ch. 4, p. 149)

Create

Help students practise coursework, understand feedback, and find suitable peer help when a topic remains difficult.

Deliver

Provide course activities and tutoring access through a proposed university partnership. Content approval and tutor oversight would need agreed responsibilities.

Capture

Use proposed licence income to support development, hosting, content review, and support, with optional secondary revenue only if validated.

Pricing and financial assumptions

The updated report does not set licence prices, premium prices, tutor charges or financial projections. These decisions require a cost estimate, a clear service scope and agreement with potential institutional partners.

Current resources are team time, existing skills and limited voluntary contributions. A live service would also need hosting, content review, tutor coordination, maintenance and user support. No financial forecast is claimed.

A standard venture with a focused difference

Quizzness is proposed as a standard venture. It combines familiar methods—gamification, online practice and peer support—around course material supplied or approved by educators. The report does not claim that this would fundamentally disrupt the market.

The core strategy is course-specific practice, immediate explanations and a direct path to screened peer support. Institutional partnerships and prototype feedback would guide expansion (Barringer & Ireland, 2019, Chapter 4).

Comparison with Duolingo

Duolingo uses interactive lessons, repeated practice and game elements. We want to use practice activities for university coursework.

Resources, costs and initial funding

The team plans to bootstrap the prototype using personal savings and sweat equity. Isaiah proposed this approach and the team agreed. Team labour, time and existing skills would reduce the need to pay external developers at this stage.

Financial contributions would be voluntary, limited, agreed and documented. Labour, responsibilities and recognition of contributions would also be recorded. No investment, loan or grant is confirmed.

Building a sustainable service

Start within the team's available skills and resources. Use prototype feedback to improve the learning and tutoring journey, and estimate the real workload of reviewed content, software maintenance and support.

Institutional partnerships, innovation initiatives and grant opportunities are the first proposed routes to additional resources. Larger external financing would be considered only after the direction, costs and responsibilities are clearer.
